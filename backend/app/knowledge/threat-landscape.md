# Threat Landscape

## Threat actors
Cybercriminals are mostly financially motivated (ransomware, fraud, data theft). Nation-state actors and advanced persistent threats (APTs) run long, targeted campaigns for espionage or disruption. Hacktivists pursue political goals. Insiders, whether malicious or careless, already have access. Script kiddies use ready-made tools with little skill. Knowing the likely actor helps decide what to defend first.

## Social engineering
Social engineering manipulates people rather than systems. Examples are phishing, pretexting (inventing a believable scenario), baiting (leaving infected USB drives), tailgating into buildings and impersonating IT support. Defence relies on verification procedures, awareness training and making it easy to report.

## Supply chain and third-party risk
Attackers compromise a supplier or software dependency to reach many victims at once, for example by poisoning a package, a build system or an update mechanism. Reduce the risk by verifying signatures, pinning dependencies, scanning with SCA tools, limiting vendor access and keeping a software bill of materials (SBOM).

## Zero-day and exploit lifecycle
A zero-day is a vulnerability that is exploited before a patch exists. After disclosure there is a window in which attackers race to exploit unpatched systems, which is why patching speed matters. Threat intelligence and the CISA KEV catalogue show what is being exploited now.

## Denial of service and data breaches
Denial of service attacks target availability. Data breaches expose confidential data, often through stolen credentials, misconfigured storage or exploited vulnerabilities. The impact includes financial loss, regulatory penalties and loss of trust, and organisations may have legal duties to notify affected people.
